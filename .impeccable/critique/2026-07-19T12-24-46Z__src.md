## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Not AI-generated-looking. The identity (Rose & Bloom, ink-on-cream, Fraunces) is committed and consistently enforced; the partner-color law (rose = me, violet = them), one motion grammar, and whisper discipline read as authored, not templated. No side-stripes, no gradient text, no hero-metric, glass used purposefully. Card radius 24 and hero radius 30 sit at the top of the range but are part of the committed romantic identity. Passes the product slop test: a user fluent in the best tools would trust this surface.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json src` → **0 findings** (clean exit). Caveat: the detector targets HTML/CSS markup; RN `StyleSheet` objects partially evade it, so the manual ban-list check above carries the weight.

**Visual overlays:** not run — no `node_modules` installed in this container, no dev server; browser evidence skipped (recorded in Run Notes).

## Overall Impression

The mechanics are award-grade and the retention architecture (morning paper / golden-hour lantern / sealed reveal) is genuinely inventive. The remaining gap is not design language — it's **trust at the seams**: pairing, SOS, and failed-write feedback are the three places where the app can silently betray the one promise it makes ("what I send reaches them").

## What's Working

- **The one-alert law on Home** — a strict priority ladder (repair > hard days > letter > hugs > date) replaces the old 8-deep stack. This is editorial restraint done properly.
- **The Us menu as a status board** — every row's subtitle turns live from data already synced ("Riya answered a question for you"), making the IA self-teaching at zero cost.
- **Motion with meaning** — ambient bloom crossfade carries presence; reduced-motion paths exist on Home; native-driver everywhere.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Pairing has no handshake.** A typo'd join code puts the two partners in parallel empty spaces, each believing they're linked; nothing ever detects it. Same-name inputs collide in `personId()` and silently merge both people into one author. **Why:** this is the app's single worst failure mode — every feature degrades into "quiet partner." **Fix:** creator writes a space-marker doc at creation; joiner subscribes and shows "Looking for Riya's space…" until a partner-authored doc appears, then celebrates "You're linked 🤍"; block `personId(name) === personId(partnerName)` in onboarding. **Command:** $impeccable harden / onboard.
2. **[P1] SOS cannot be recalled.** After the confirm dialog the partner's phone alarms; a mis-tap at 3am cries wolf on the one feature that must never. **Fix:** 5-second "Sending… tap to cancel" hold-off (undo-send pattern) before the write + push. **Command:** $impeccable harden.
3. **[P1] Optimistic success, invisible failure.** `send()` toasts "Hug on its way 🤍" before `db.add` resolves; a `false` result (rules rejection, quota, oversize) is swallowed. **Fix:** thread the boolean through for high-stakes writes (SOS, letters, deck answers, moments) — corrective toast or a "pending" mark on the row. **Command:** $impeccable harden.
4. **[P2] Home's evening stack.** Alert + paper + hearth + coach + ribbon + reveal + feed + whisper + canvas + on-this-day ≈ 9 blocks. Once tonight's reveal is opened, collapse the Reveal card into a feed row; fold the canvas card into the feed unless partner strokes are waiting. Target ≤ 6 blocks. **Command:** $impeccable distill.
5. **[P2] The joiner's vocabulary debt.** Six coined nouns, one coach mark. The second partner gets no introduction to lantern/ember/capsule/seal/paper. **Fix:** reuse the existing `@tether/seen/*` one-time-hint machinery: first time each ritual surfaces, one dismissible line. **Command:** $impeccable onboard / clarify.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer — the joining partner):** join-code field doesn't auto-format groups (ABCD-EFGH…), no feedback that the code found anything; "Begin our space" succeeds identically for a right or wrong code. Will believe they're paired when they aren't. The six coined ritual nouns arrive unexplained.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** state persistence is excellent (AsyncStorage everywhere, optimistic writes). But after day one there is no one-tap hug — the warmest action needs Home → Miss You → grid. SOS sits at the very top of Miss You, out of one-handed thumb reach (defensible as deliberate friction; decide, don't drift).

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** `textFaint` (#A89F9B ≈ 2.6:1 on white) is still used for the Onboarding date placeholder ("Tap to choose a date") and LevelSelector end labels — the exact placeholder-contrast bug ui.tsx's Field already fixed. Onboarding breathe loop and Miss You emoji burst don't check `prefersReducedMotion()` (Home does). Fixed px font sizes: verify layouts at 200% OS font scale.

## Minor Observations

- Chevron color: `colors.primary` in Home feed vs `textFaint` in Us menu — pick one.
- Empty TimeCapsule orb reads as decorative glass until tapped; its toast is the only teacher.
- Onboarding prefills "Piyush"/"Riya" as literal state — the joiner may keep the wrong "your name."
- ProgressBar clamps to 2% minimum — shows progress where there is none.
- Resolved Reveal card copy ("Read them again") keeps gold-card weight for a done state.

## Questions to Consider

- What confirms the *first* successful sync between the two phones — and could that be the most celebrated moment in the app?
- Should the hearth own a persistent one-tap hug, making the core loop (open → feel → send) a 2-second gesture?
- When both partners have done everything today, what does Home look like? Is there a designed "full" state as intentional as the empty one?
